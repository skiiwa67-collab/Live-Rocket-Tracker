# Red-team: DESIGN-beacon-1.md (Skippy, 2026-10-14), defensive test scenarios only
This is abuse of our own feature, written as synthetic drills with mitigations. Every mitigation is a setting with a safe default and an adjustable starting value (warn, don't clamp). § = DESIGN-beacon-1.md.

## Ranked items
1. **Coercive control: manual SOS reaches an abusive family member** (§8 BC-19, §2 FAMILY). Scenario: the victim's family list includes the abuser, the victim presses SOS, and the abuser gets an exact location. Mitigation: `beacon.safeFamily` (a subset of contacts that get beacons, picked at setup) plus `beacon.excludeContacts` (default empty, per-contact). Manual SOS goes to safe family + verified responders only. When there's no safe-family list yet, first-run setup asks once and lets the user skip. Drill: an excluded contact receives nothing from manual SOS or auto-fire.
2. **The chirp reveals a person hiding** (§4 chirp, §6 last row). The silent toggle is reachable only from the countdown, and a manual SOS has no countdown, so a hiding person can't find it under stress. Mitigation: a discreet SOS path, `beacon.discreetSosGesture` (e.g. a separate "Silent SOS" button or a different long-press). It never chirps and gives no vibration. Add `beacon.manualChirp` (default OFF for manual SOS; auto-incapacitation keeps chirping ON) and `beacon.chirpStartDelayMin` (start 0 auto / n/a manual). Drill: Silent SOS → radio beacon sent, zero sound or vibration.
3. **The abuser silently turns it OFF** (§5 `enableRequiresAuth` lowers without auth; §7). The owner notification lands on the same phone the abuser holds and can be dismissed. Mitigation: `beacon.notifyFamilyOnChange` goes to *safe family only* (default ON when a safe-family list exists), a 7-day reminder instead of 30 (`periodicReminderDays`, start 7), and the change log is visible only behind the owner's auth.
4. **The abuser knows the PIN and raises the tier** (§5 auth on raise). Auth can't tell the owner from someone who knows the PIN. Mitigation: `beacon.duressPin` (optional; it appears to work but leaves the tier unchanged and quietly alerts safe family). Raising to EVERYONE also needs a 24 h cool-off (`beacon.everyoneCoolOffH`, start 24, warn below 1) with a reminder to the owner.
5. **The "armed" indicator itself tips off an abuser** (§7: persistent notification + status-bar icon). Mitigation: `beacon.discreetStatus` (default OFF) switches to a neutral icon or no icon. The safe default keeps the visible icon, since it guards against secret enabling. **Flag for Chris (Q2).**
6. **Distance-band triangulation** (§2 Level 0 band, §3 bearing + band). Several Level 0 observers combine their bands to pin a person. Mitigation: `beacon.level0BandMi` coarse (start 0.25 mi), band edges snapped to a fixed grid so moving observers learn nothing new, updates per firing interval only (no per-request refresh), and no bearing for Level 0 (`beacon.level0Bearing` OFF).
7. **Looters find people who can't move** (§2 EVERYONE, §7). Even a band plus "incapacitated" tells someone a person is helpless. Mitigation: the Level 0 text says "someone nearby asked for help — go with others or alert responders" with no incapacitation detail (`beacon.level0ShowStatus` OFF). Auto-fire at EVERYONE drops to RESPONDERS outside an active disaster alert (`beacon.everyoneOnlyDuringAlert` ON).
8. **An attacker stops the beacon on a helpless person** (§5 `stopRequiresPin` OFF). Mitigation: stopping without auth sends one final signed "stopped on device, not confirmed OK" to safe family and responders (`beacon.notifyOnUnauthStop` ON), and the recipients keep the last location. An "I'm OK" confirmed with auth sends "OK". **Flag for Chris (Q3).**
9. **Fake beacons as lures** (§7, BC-11). The key is self-made per phone, so a valid signature proves nothing about who a stranger is. Mitigation: Level 0 shows unverified *and* stranger-signed beacons as "help request reported" with the band only and a forward-to-responders action. No navigate-to button for Level 0 (`beacon.level0NavigateTo` OFF). Family and responders verify against known keys or credentials.
10. **False incapacitation: battery drain and location leak** (§1 held-then-still, desk pattern only flat). A phone in a bag, on a car seat or in a drawer fires. Mitigation: `beacon.maxAutoFiresPerDay` (start 2, then countdown doubles), `beacon.autoFireTierCap` (auto-fire uses RESPONDERS at most unless a disaster alert is active), add "in pocket or bag" (proximity + dark) as a cancel hint, and the countdown also vibrates a paired watch if present.
11. **Replay or reuse within the 30 min window** (§7 `maxAgeMin`=30, rotating ID). A replayed beacon still shows as current for up to 30 min. Mitigation: a per-firing monotonic counter + nonce, receivers reject an older counter for the same ID, and show the age always ("3 min old"), not just "stale" past the limit.
12. **Mesh flood from many keys** (§7, BC-13). The per-key rate limit doesn't stop many fresh keys. Mitigation: new keys get lone weight, beacons are aggregated per cell before relay, known family/contact keys get relay priority over strangers, and `beacon.meshStrangerMaxPerCellPerHour` starts at 20, labelled when hit.
Also (minor): the responder fallback to Level 0 (§3) should also drop when the revocation-list age exceeds `responder.revocationMaxAgeH` (start 24).

## Flags for Chris (3)
- **Q1:** default delivery of manual SOS when no safe-family list is set: all family (current, BC-19) or ask at first SOS setup?
- **Q2:** armed indicator: keep it always visible (protects against secret enabling) or allow a discreet option (protects a victim hiding the feature from an abuser)?
- **Q3:** an unauthenticated stop: send "stopped, not confirmed OK" to recipients (protects the helpless) even though it could tell an abusive recipient the victim stopped it?
