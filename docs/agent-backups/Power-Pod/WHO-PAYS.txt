================================================================
THE RULE
================================================================
Carriers and retailers will not absorb metro-scale solar, batteries,
landing-gear strips, or phone/vehicle mesh radios. LTL runs on pennies
a mile. A Walmart DC will not volunteer to become a city power plant
on its own capex.

So you do not ask them to.

Government pays for the MESH and the POWER NODES because both are a
strategic asset:
  - 911 mode on phones + emergency radios (information mesh)
  - warehouse / trailer / strip / pack nodes (energy mesh)

When the grid dies, yards still have kWh and phones still talk.
That is dual-use infrastructure. Same class as a highway, a levee,
or STARLINK kits for disaster comms. Not a gift to a trucking company.

The company gets a dock that still works.
The country gets a distributed plant and a comms mesh it did not have
to permit as a new power station or a new cell network.

================================================================
WHAT GOVERNMENT BUYS (the plant, not the freight)
================================================================
Pays (THIS project, the power node):
  - rooftop / trailer-skin PV at NODE-CLASS sites
  - building packs + snap-in trailer modules at those sites
  - landing-gear strips (the bus)

Does NOT pay here:
  - radios. Phones and emergency-vehicle radios already exist.
    That mesh is the 911 project. Do not put it in the trucking HUD.

Does not pay (unless a later program says so):
  - the company's freight, drivers, or tractors
  - inventing a new map app to replace Google/TomTom
  - every warehouse in the 1.1B SF DFW market

Node-class first: named LTL docks and big DCs we already have numbers
for. Estes DFW four yards are the prototype. Do not spray panels on
every industrial roof and call it strategy.

The logic is first-principles:
  A warehouse service drop cannot charge a fleet. Already locked.

  That plant is useless as a private science project and valuable as
  a city node when ERCOT North is in trouble or a quake kills cell.
  Public pays for public-option capacity. Private uses it every day
  so it is maintained.
